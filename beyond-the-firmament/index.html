Who I am

My name is Aivars, but most people call me Archi. I was born in Latvia and have lived in Dublin for almost nineteen years. I came to art as a mature student, studying Fine Art at NCAD in Dublin, and I paint, write and build an archive called Spirit of Time.

I was never drawn to mysticism or esoteric subjects. If anything, I kept away from them. The coincidences started during my years at NCAD, and they came with such force and in such strange sequences that they changed how I see the world. I did not go looking for these experiences. In many ways, they found me.

How I work with it

For about five years I have kept dated photographs, screenshots, messages, paintings and dictated accounts. With the help of AI I sort them into event cards: what happened, when it was first recorded, when I told it, when I later understood it, and when it was published. Each card carries a confidence rating for the source, the date and the connection. I try to write the most modest claim the evidence supports, and also the claim it does not support.

To me it sometimes feels like electricity. I cannot see the thing itself, only what it does. I would like to understand it, not just believe in it.

Where it began: Dorset Street

The first and strongest of my synchronicities happened on 29 and 30 November 2021, on a corner of Dorset Street in Dublin, on my way to NCAD. On the first morning I photographed a man with trembling hands sitting on the steps. The next morning, at the same spot, I saw a man I remembered from a boutique in Riga around 2000, and had recognised twice since in Dublin. While I photographed him, my own hands were trembling.

Two different men, one place, two mornings, and five photographs with the times in their file names.

It felt like crossing a threshold, and knowing I could not go back.

I can return to that state in an instant, even now, and I would never mistake it for anything else. Experiences of that strength are rare for me, perhaps once a year. Everything that came after, including the evening with Marcus, I measure against it.

    The engraving and its three observers

    The Flammarion engraving, first published in 1888, is well known in mystical and philosophical circles, so finding it on your page is not unlikely in itself. What struck me was the route by which it came back to me. In my life the image has its own history:

    2020

    Derek

    I came to anthroposophy through a coincidence of its own: I found the Goetheanum twice, by two different routes, before I knew what it was. That path led me to the anthroposophy site anthroposophy.eu and to its founder, Derek. We began writing to each other, and for more than a year and a half our correspondence about Jung, synchronicity and anthroposophy has made many ideas clearer for me. Since 15 May 2020 the Flammarion engraving has been his Facebook profile picture. That is where I took it from.

    31 May

    Me

    I put myself inside the engraving holding a mobile phone: a person of our time trying to look past the edge of the known world with technology. It became my avatar and part of my artistic identity.

    2026

    You

    Your article of 20 October 2026, Why the Increase in Global Reports of Mystical Experiences?, carries the same engraving. It was the first image I saw when I opened your page on the night of 19 November.

    Why this engraving matters to me

    At first the engraving was foreign to me. It was simply Derek's picture. Over time it entered my own world: a couple of my synchronicities were connected with it, and I made it my sign by placing myself inside it. So it is not just an image I like. It marks a whole line of my life: the Goetheanum, anthroposophy, Derek, and the questions we have shared.

    When I typed your name into Facebook and the first thing I saw was this engraving, my own avatar, I understood perfectly well that it was chance. But it was meaningful chance, the kind your work asks us to notice.

    A change in register

    Something has also shifted. My earlier coincidences were mostly about events: a person, a place, a photograph. This one, and the evening with Marcus, are more about meaning: a symbol returning through different people, each time with a new layer added to it.

    The coincidences seem to have moved into another register, from what happens to what it means. That shift is part of what I would most like to understand.

    Without planning it, I then did what my picture shows: I used technology, an AI, to look for people working at the edge of ordinary explanation. It led me to a researcher whose page shows the wanderer looking through the sky.

    The structure that keeps repeating

    It is not one strange coincidence that keeps me working on this. It is that many of them share the same structure:

    1 · An impulse

    I do something unplanned and unusual for me: I pick up an object, take a photograph, make an image, write a song.

    2 · An outside event

    Something happens that I did not arrange and could not know about: a message, a meeting, a discovery.

    3 · A specific match

    The two meet in a recognisable person, object, place, title or symbol, not just a general mood.

    4 · The past returns

    The theme turns out to have been present years before, and the earlier act takes on a new meaning.

    Two variants. In the first, an image comes first and reality answers later (the dream and the Goetheanum; the stage set of Red). In the second, a spontaneous act comes shortly before an outside event with the same content (Dorset Street; the ashtray and Marcus).

    What I do not claim. A repeating structure in my stories does not prove there is one mechanism behind the events. Part of what repeats may be how my own mind notices and links things. I count that as worth studying too. I also know I do not record the many evenings when nothing happened.

    A parallel I cannot ignore

    I was as far from AI as I was from mysticism. Yet the two entered my life in the same years. My first strong coincidences came in 2020–2021, during my studies. When Midjourney appeared in 2022, I started using it at once, and then ChatGPT from its very first days, followed by other systems. The two lines have grown up together ever since, and without these tools I would not be where I am now.

    I have tested each of them on myself: how they flatter, how they can steer attention, how a beautiful image appears in seconds and how deep the disappointment can be afterwards. In Steiner's language I try to keep a Michaelic balance between the two temptations.

    What grew out of it

    Out of this work came my Codex, the core archive from which I am now writing a book. Many synchronicities are still unwritten. I do not yet know what they are for. I let them show themselves in the process, and I keep the record as honest as I can.

    This is also why your article on the algorithmic gaze matters to me: in my case, the self-observer and the machine have been working side by side from the start.

    Your words that met me

    “Meaningful coincidences are suggestions, not demands.”

    When I reached this sentence in your October article, I cried. For years I felt as if I were in a dark room that was slowly filling with light. I could start to see outlines, but I had nothing to hold on to. Your sentence describes what I did that night: I took the coincidence as an invitation to look further, not as an order.

    And the algorithmic gaze

    Then I read The Algorithmic Gaze and the Self-Observer (July 2025). It applies to me directly. An AI did not cause the evening with Marcus, but it did take part in what followed, because it suggested your name. So in my archive I record where each link came from, and I try to keep my own inner observer rather than let a machine strengthen what I already believe.

    Both articles hold the same position, and it is one I share: be open to the unusual, but stay free.
