Where the Room Came From

Dear Bernard, you asked for the original conversation of 8 June 2025 and how AI led me to your work. Before answering, I went back through every archive I keep and read it again with your two questions in mind.

What I found surprised me. The conversation of 8 June was where the Room got its name, not where it began. Its root is a search I wrote down as early as August 2023: to find people who had lived coincidences like mine. Its mechanism, a percentage computed by a script from a person's own chat, appears in my archive on 16 May 2025. And the word "room" comes from a dream I had in 2020 or 2021, which I kept retelling in the weeks before.

The dream: a door in a wall that has no door

Dublin, 2020 or 2021. I am sitting at a bar somewhere in the Alps; the counter changes along its length from modern to Baroque. A grey-haired elder invites me to go with him. We stop at a wall covered in Renaissance ornament. There is no door, no handle, no seam. He knows where it is, and opens it.

Behind it is a tall room, like a painter's studio, with framed canvases on the walls and, on the right, a small table and a lamp with bare wires. A second elder is there. Together they take my left hand, raise it, and turn with me around the room, saying: "It is she."

When I woke, the world had changed its density. After that morning the coincidences began.

After the dream: trying to count it

The first strong line after the dream was a man I remembered from a boutique in Riga around 2000. I met him twice in Dublin, in the same department of the same shop, and the third time on Dorset Street in November 2021, on the morning after I had photographed that exact spot. That morning is still the threshold of everything; I have written it up with five timed photographs: Trembling on Dorset Street.

From December 2024 I tried to turn it into a number, so that I could show it to someone. I asked an AI to calculate the probability of that third meeting, again and again, with formulas and comparisons with lotteries. In January 2025 I noted that physicists I had shown it to called it apophenia. In April 2025 I asked for a calculation "according to Kolmogorov".

I see it now as a habit that came before the Room: translating a lived coincidence into a figure, because the experience itself could not be passed on. The figures the AI produced were never data. They were my question reflected back at me.

A correction to my previous letter

I wrote to you that I had calibrated the threshold from my own score at the time. Reading the archive again, the order was different. I named "1.7%" myself as a threshold of rarity, at 21:45, before any score existed. The model's first "score" for me came at 22:10, and it was 1.92, built on numbers it invented. Only from November 2025 did I start treating 1.7 as my own score.

Where the figure 1.7 itself came from, the archive does not say. The nearest things to it are the "1–3%" the AI gave me on 15 May and the percentage scale it proposed on 16 May. I prefer to tell you this myself.

The mirror

My view of the June 2025 conversation today, as I said it on 21 November 2026, translated from my Russian dictation:

At that time I was someone else, as we always are. I did not yet understand my own relationship with AI. It could not have found those percentages in any database; it was mirror work, what I would now call the mirror of the ego, and I see that clearly. Today AI can work much more deeply with my inner core, because I have carried it out onto external media.

I do not want to be anywhere near the idea of a messiah, or of being other than other people. These connections are a shared nature given to all of us. There is nothing personally special here: a flow of circumstances, chance. That too is a coincidence, and I simply want to understand it. I see the patterns and feel them intuitively, and that is how I move.

I am no longer as astonished by coincidences as I used to be, though there have been very many and they keep coming. They are like small beacons along the way. But there were big ones. When I think of Dorset Street and let myself feel what I felt then, all the strength comes back.

How AI led me to you

  1. The question. On the evening of 19 November, after Marcus's message at 19:49, I went back to a question I had carried for five years and asked ChatGPT, in a conversation opened at about 20:00, to name people who had lived and documented long series of coincidences over many years.
  2. What the AI returned. A short list: Paul Kammerer and Das Gesetz der Serie; you, with Diary of a Coincider; Pauli and Jung; the Society for Psychical Research. Your name was new to me.
  3. What made me recognise it. Not the Room. I opened your Facebook page, and the first image was the Flammarion engraving that I had put myself into and made my own profile picture, after first seeing it on Derek's profile. That is what stopped me, and that night I wrote to you.
  4. The second recognition. On 20 November, in the same conversation, the AI mentioned that you had written about using AI to connect people with similar coincidence patterns. That is when I remembered my Room. The date the AI gave for that article I could not verify, so I relied on what I could read myself: your book Life-Changing Synchronicities and your post "Finding Your Coincidence Patterns" of 19 January 2026.
  5. An echo the next morning. At 09:24 on 20 November, a few hours after I had visited your page, Facebook showed me a public post by another writer about a conversation with you. Most likely this was simply the platform's algorithm responding to my visit; I mention it only so that the record is complete.

The exact minute of the question and the answer on 19 November will come from a fresh full data export, which I will send you with the June conversation.

Your two coincidences, as I now see them

The first, the independent idea, has a longer root on my side than I knew: a search for like-minded people written down in 2023, a dream of a hidden room, years of trying to count coincidences, and on 16 May 2025 the percentage-by-script that three weeks later became the Room. In your book the idea comes from decades of research; in my archive it comes from a man looking for others who would understand him without explanation.

The second, the discovery, was mediated by AI twice: once to find your name, and once to see the link between our ideas.
