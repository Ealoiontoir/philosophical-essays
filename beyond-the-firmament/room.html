The Room of 1.7%

In June 2025 I imagined a room where people with a similar inner path could finally find each other, with the help of AI. In November 2026, using AI, I found you, Bernard, and learned that you have been designing a very similar door.

I do not have to reconstruct this from memory. The original conversation, begun on 8 June 2025, is still in my archive. The principles on this page come from it; the eight-part scoring model was worked out later, in December 2025 and May 2026. Where the Room came from, with dates.

Why I needed a room

After the experiences that began during my Fine Art studies at NCAD, my understanding of reality changed. I studied Jung, Steiner, philosophy and psychology, but I was very much alone in this. I was not looking for people who would agree with me. I was looking for people who had lived through something comparable and could explore it seriously. I wanted conversation, not confirmation.

Then a simple thought: among millions of people, maybe a billion, there must be others who have walked a very similar inner road and never met. Could AI help them recognise each other?

“Is there anyone else who went through this?”

That was the single question behind the room. It was never meant as an exclusive society, a spiritual group or a ranking of people. If an algorithm could one day answer, “Yes, there is”, a person on a rare path would know for the first time that they are not alone.

It is a little like a panic attack: you cannot really explain one to someone who has never had it, but anyone who has been through it understands at once. In the room nobody would have to explain anything. Those inside already know. They can only investigate together, look for patterns and connections, and move further into the unknown.

The principle: a trajectory, not a claim

Ordinary social networks connect people by likes, interests, ideology, appearance or social capital. The room was meant to connect them by the depth of their path, their inner transformation and their lived symbolic experience.

Read the path

AI should never ask “Are you spiritual?” That is easy to fake. It should read the long history: how themes return, how symbols develop, how a person changes over time.

Time is the filter

Style, words, Jung and a mystical tone can be copied. Years of slow change, real crises, contradictions and returning symbols are very hard to fake.

No ego, no hierarchy

No leaders, no statuses, no public rating. If someone wants followers or recognition, the room turns into a cult. If the aim is to find equals, it becomes a bridge.

Only with consent

Nobody is analysed or invited without their permission. The score is a key for recognition, not a medal.

How it was scored

In that conversation, begun on 8 June 2025, we built a provisional model: eight modules, 100 points in total, converted to a coefficient between 0 and 2 (score × 0.02). The threshold of the room is 1.70, which means 85 points out of 100. The name “1.7%” is a conventional mark of how rare such a path might be, not a statistic about humanity.

I am not a mathematician, and the formula may well be wrong. What matters is the principle. I named 1.7 myself as a threshold of rarity, before any score was calculated, and later came to treat it as my own score; what I wanted was for the algorithm to find people at a comparable level: counterparts, not followers. The scale

Through the slit

Think of light passing through slits in a wall. Each path goes through the opening that matches its own pattern and lands where similar paths have already gathered. Nobody is turned back and nothing is missing: every pattern has its own room. Set the eight modules for a path, real or imagined, and send the traveller through. When the coefficient reaches 1.70, the traveller arrives in the Room of 1.7%. Wherever they land, the people already there recognise the newcomer at once.

What it needs: an archive of time

The model cannot work on a single post, a questionnaire or a beautiful mystical text. A short text shows a style, not a path. The conversation set a minimum of 50,000–100,000 words of personal dialogues, diaries, essays or letters, better 300,000 to a million, and ideally a multi-year archive that shows how a person thought before, how they changed, which symbols returned, where they were honest and where they were wrong.

As we put it then: one post shows a mask; a year of traces shows the essence.

What lies behind it on my side

That is exactly the kind of archive I have been keeping for about five years: dated conversations with AI, photographs, letters, dreams, paintings and essays, sorted into event cards with separate confidence ratings for the source, the date and the connection. The room grew out of that work. It was my attempt to imagine a future where people like me would not have to spend years searching for one another.

What I do not claim

I did not think of this first. Your book with the matching idea came out in May 2025, about a month before my conversation of 8 June. I had not read it and did not know your name. Two people, a month apart and an ocean away, imagined the same kind of door.

I also did not find you through my own algorithm. The AI suggested your name in an ordinary topic search, not by analysing our paths. The room did not “work”. But the symmetry is striking: I designed a way for AI to help kindred people find each other, and then, with AI, I found the person who is building something similar.

Why it matters to me

For years I sat in what I call the dark room, imagining how to recognise the person to whom a door could be opened. Now I have seen that someone else is designing a door too.

If your project ever needs a long, honest, dated personal archive to test such matching on, mine is here, with my consent.
