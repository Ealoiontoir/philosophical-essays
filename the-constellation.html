The Constellation

The whole fieldseven hundred years, thirty-two beacons
The fourth axis · the order in which they arrived12 June 2026 — all of them
The four axes of attraction

Seen from above, the figures do not form one mass. Four distinct lines come through. It is not a strict classification — authors cross the axes — but each holds one as its main one. What matters is that the axes do not duplicate each other: axis I without axis II is immersion without sobriety; axis III without axis IV is metaphysics without pain; axis II without axis I is coldness with nothing warm behind it.

Axis I

Inner experience and image

Jung · Proust · Tarkovsky · Rothko. How the inner becomes real. The image as a unit of thinking. The archive of memory as architecture.

Axis II

Ethics, history, catastrophe

Arendt · Jaspers · Benjamin · Brecht. How to think after the break. The cold instrument of distinction. Distance as an ethical position.

Axis III

Metaphysics of form and becoming

Goethe · Steiner · Heidegger · Novalis. Thinking as process, not as result. Form as a path of knowledge. Spirit in matter.

Axis IV

Moral and existential rupture

Tolstoy · Kafka. Rupture as a creative source. The impossibility of an exit as a form of honesty. The warning that an archive can be a trap.

The map of tensions

The gravest danger for a constellation built by resonance is that it becomes an ornament — beautiful, symmetrical, safe; a system of mirrors that only confirm each other. Against that works another principle: the map must show the fractures as well as the closenesses. Five figures burn warm in the field because they break the logic the centre prefers. Fly to any of them for what exactly they break.

How to move

Drag to turn the field. The wheel carries you through the scales, and the line at the lower left names where you are. Click a figure and the camera flies to it. The tour follows the documented chains — Eckhart to Böhme to Novalis to Steiner, then Hilma to Kant to Jung to Pauli, then the witnesses and the five that work against the centre. The slider along the foot replays the order in which the analyses were actually written, between 15 April and 12 June 2026.

A beacon is not a lighthouse that steers you off the rocks. It is a figure who shows you, by existing, that the territory you are moving through is navigable.
