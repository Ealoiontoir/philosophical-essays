The Diamond Rule
Virtue, Conscience
and the Ethics of the Noocene

An essay-map after Michael Epstein, Robert Sardello and the twelve virtues

The impulse for this essay came after watching Michael Epstein speak on the Diamond Rule of ethics — where virtue, conscience and the relation to the uniqueness of the other person became the ethical spark. Epstein is not a source of a quotation here but a point of entry, through which a larger theme opened: virtue · conscience · the measure between extremes · the Diamond Rule · the Noocene · a new ethics of consciousness.

Robert Sardello

The Twelve Virtues

Virtues are not moral prohibitions but organs of perception of the soul. Through them a person begins to see the world differently. Tap each for its link to the Diamond Rule and to the Noocene.

Robert Sardello · on the virtues of the soul

The Power of Soul

The list goes back to Blavatsky, was confirmed by Steiner, and is linked to the twelve zodiac signs — the rhythms of the living cosmos. The "gateway" virtue is Discernment.

Codex Notes

Archive notes

Where the idea came from

Through Epstein's talk on the Diamond Rule. The impulse coincided with a phase of work on freedom, responsibility and the discernment of motives — the Michaelic discipline.

Link to the Codex

The node converges with the archive's five motifs (Fissure, Mirror, Threshold) and with the analyzed AI layer of Epstein: the diamond rule is what cannot be delegated to the logoid in the mentaur.

Why it matters

In an age where technique can be taught to imitate depth, the virtue of discernment (Discernment ≡ conscience) becomes the defense of the authenticity of inner work.

How to develop it further

A separate "Syntellect" node; translating the twelve virtues into daily exercises; a passage through Kierkegaard; Beuys's "social sculpture" as virtue in action.

Do what no one can do in your place — and let conscience not let you believe that you have already done it.
