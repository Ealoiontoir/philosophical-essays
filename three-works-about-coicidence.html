Gallery · Archive of the Codex · Dublin · November 2021

Three Works
About One Person

A series of paintings made during the period when the same man appeared across different cities, different spaces, across twenty years.

He appeared in Riga, then in Dublin, then on a street --
in the same spot where, the day before, a stranger's hands had trembled.
I · The Series

Works About This Person

Three works made in the same period — by the same hands, in the same search. The paintings are not illustrations of the event. They are a parallel channel: the same movement of fingers that on the morning of 30 November were trembling over a camera.

Formats: two canvases 40×40 cm, one charcoal 86×39 cm, and one 80×80 cm in progress for four years — repainted, covered, reopened. The subject keeps slipping away. Perhaps that is the subject.

Work I The First Appearance 40 × 40 cm · Oil on canvas

The first encounter. Brown Thomas, Grafton Street, Dublin — approximately 2019. A quarter century after the boutique in Riga. Recognition happened before consciousness could verify it: the body remembered before memory produced its evidence.

Work II The Second Appearance 40 × 40 cm · Oil on canvas

About a year later — Brown Thomas again, nearly the same spot. I pointed him out to my daughter. The second time changed the structure: a coincidence becomes a motif. Something returns, but does not explain why.

Work III The Third Appearance · Dorset Street 86 × 39 cm · Charcoal on canvas, varnished with oil

30 November 2021, ~10:25. Not in a shop — on a street. The corner of Dorset Street and Blessington Street. Pressed into a doorway, surrounded by black rubbish bags, moving back and forth in a loop. The same signature of presence memorised in Riga a quarter century ago. And when recognition happened — my hands began to tremble.

Work IV The Subject That Slips Away · Four Years in Progress 80 × 80 cm · Oil on canvas

I have been working on this painting for four years. It changes constantly — repainted, covered, reopened. I cannot catch the subject. Something keeps slipping from my hands. Each time I think I have it, it moves. Perhaps that is the subject. Left: current state. Right: an earlier state.

II · Visualisation

A Constellation of Coincidences · Quantum Model

In quantum mechanics, a particle does not occupy a fixed position until observed — it exists as a cloud of probabilities. These five events are not points on a timeline. They are atoms of one molecule. Hover over each node to reveal its connections and weight.

Jung called this an acausal connecting principle: not A causes B, but A and B are expressions of the same hidden structure. The constellation has no centre. Each atom draws all others toward itself — with different forces, on different orbits.
III · Probability Theory

The Architecture of the Impossible

Nine filters. Each one eliminates the greater part of possible universes in which this event does not exist.

P₁
Memory retention of a casual customer after 20+ years
1 / 200
P₂
Relocation from Latvia to Dublin specifically
1 / 70
P₃
Encounter at the same store on the same visit
1 / 500
P₄
Behavioural signature distinct enough to recognise after 20+ years
1 / 3
P₅
Second encounter at the same location within one year
1 / 20
P₆₋₈
Location cluster: Dorset St, correct block, morning window (one filter)
1 / 50
P₉
Both events documented photographically with precise metadata
1 / 100
≈ 5 × 10⁻¹³

One event per two trillion. Even if every estimate is wrong by a factor of ten in every direction — we remain in the zone of events that occur, at best, once in several generations. Not "rare." A different category.

◎
The Earth as Target

Cover the entire surface of the Earth with a target. Drop a stone blindly from space. The probability of hitting a specific A4 sheet of paper lying on a lawn in suburban Dublin — is comparable to the probability of this event.

♠
The Deck of Cards

A deck was shuffled in Riga in 2000. In Dublin in 2021, a new deck was purchased and shuffled — and the first twelve cards appeared in the same order. Not "some" sequence. A repetition of the specific pattern across twenty years and 2,500 kilometres.

This attempt to measure the event with mathematics is not a way of explaining it. It is a way of testing it to breaking point: to see whether it will crumble under the pressure of a number. It did not crumble.

Yesterday, his hands trembled. Today — mine.

Dublin · November 2021 — May 2026
